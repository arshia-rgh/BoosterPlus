## Round one and coverage

The actual components were independently rendered from the synthetic local preview at `http://127.0.0.1:4197/?page=login-customer`, `login-admin` and `login-booster`.

- Chromium: all three portals at 1440 × 1000 and 390 × 844.
- Chromium shared customer modal: narrow 320 × 568, tablet 768 × 1024 and short viewport 390 × 420.
- WebKit shared customer modal: 390 × 844 and 390 × 420.
- All eleven cases: opening, initial focus, forward/reverse keyboard navigation, Escape, close button, focus return, draft retention, dimensions and horizontal overflow.
- Six supplemental cases: viewport-only screenshots, explicit inert-background focus attempts and scrolling to the final action. WebKit Option/Alt+Tab was also used to include buttons under its default macOS keyboard-navigation behavior.

The modal is clear and legible, with a visible heading, close control, current/new email labels and primary action. The native overlay separates recovery from sign-in without expanding the login card. Card width and height were exactly unchanged in every measured case. On phones, scrolling to the trigger changed the card's viewport Y coordinate; this is page scrolling, not card growth.

At 320 px, the heading wraps cleanly and the dialog keeps 12 px side margins. Neither the page nor dialog overflows horizontally in any case. The short 390 × 420 viewport keeps the modal within 12 px top/bottom margins, and internal scrolling reaches the complete primary button. The 320 × 568 case also scrolls internally as needed. The close control remains reachable by scrolling to the header or keyboard navigation, and Escape closes from the scrolled state.

Current account email receives initial focus in all eleven cases. Escape and the visible close control restore focus to the recovery trigger. Both typed email drafts persist after close/reopen. Native modality prevents focus on the underlying trigger; keyboard navigation never reaches a background page control. At the native browser-chrome boundary `document.activeElement` reports BODY while `document.hasFocus()` is false, then returns to the dialog. This is not a focus escape into the page. Consequently, the initial raw harness's strict `focusContained`/`reverseFocusContained` booleans alone are not an application failure; the supplemental observations distinguish browser chrome from an active background control.

No actionable visual or interaction findings were identified; no fix/review round trip was required. No script errors, external requests or non-GET attempts occurred in the main run. Requests were restricted to local GETs, and no recovery send action was submitted.
