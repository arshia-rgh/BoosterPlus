# Staff availability and play-together sessions

SQL migration 13 owns staff profiles, dated availability, bookings and immutable booking events. Ent generates queries only. The existing worker sends durable reference-only reminders; no separate queue or runtime migration is introduced.

Staff declare an IANA time zone, optional game/region tags and an enabled calendar. Tags guide manual assignment and grant no permissions. Staff manage their own calendar; administrators can manage another active staff member after recent verification of both factors. Dated windows are 15 minutes to 12 hours, begin within 90 days, cannot overlap, and are bounded to 200 active future windows per staff member. Removing a window or pausing a calendar requires resolving its future confirmed sessions first. Existing window instants remain unchanged when the profile zone changes.

A customer with a paid, assigned play-together order can propose an interval inside current declared availability. A proposal reserves no capacity. Staff confirmation rechecks the current assignment, account permissions, payment, calendar, availability and overlap with either the staff member's or customer's confirmed sessions. The identity transaction fence serializes confirmation with competing mutations; adjacent intervals are allowed. Slot responses expose free intervals only, without customer identities, order IDs or occupied booking details. Weekly selection and cursor pagination bound queries.

Inputs contain local minute precision, an IANA zone and an explicit first/second occurrence for repeated local clock times. Nonexistent local times are rejected. The resolver inspects actual zone intervals, including non-hour clock changes; the pinned Go runtime embeds time-zone data. Persisted UTC instants and the submitted zone make later display independent of server settings.

Proposals use private request hashes and UUID idempotency keys. Reusing a key with different input fails. Confirmation, cancellation and attendance use optimistic revisions. Rescheduling atomically cancels the original and creates a linked replacement proposal; it does not silently retain the original reservation. Histories and order timelines remain visible to authorized parties. Attendance and no-show recording become available after the scheduled start, with no automatic fees, refund, penalty or payout implications. Those commercial rules require owner decisions.

Reassignment and reviewed completion cancel proposed and future/ongoing confirmed sessions in the same transaction as the work change. Former staff immediately lose order/session access. Historical ended sessions remain available for administrator attendance review. Starting play-together work requires a confirmed current-assignment session that has not ended, permitting preparation before its start. Account-mode work continues to require its separate temporary credential handoff.

The worker claims upcoming confirmed sessions once within one hour of their start and transactionally queues customer/staff notices. Delivery rechecks booking state, payment, assignment, staff authority and notification preferences. Cancellation or reassignment suppresses pending reminders. SMTP remains at least once; an ambiguous provider response can duplicate a generic email, and changes after the final authorization check cannot retract accepted mail. No private notes, credentials or other customers' schedules appear in email/event payloads.

Calendar and booking mutations have atomic audit records. Audit failure rolls back availability, booking state and timeline changes. HTTP routes use existing origin/CSRF, authentication, role, input-size and shared rate protections. Customer and booster interfaces exclude commercial data outside their established order permissions.

This stage supplies manual staffing and dated sessions. Recurring availability, automatic assignment, approved no-show/dispute policy, calendar-aware delivery calculations, proof uploads and explicit booster conversation sharing remain separate work. It does not enable live payment settlement or make production-capacity claims.
